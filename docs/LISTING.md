## Short description

Use the manifest description as it is:

> Turn Canvas into a mind-mapping tool: collapse and move whole branches, auto layout in any direction, keyboard creation and navigation, map styles, an editable outline, snapshots, and export to FreeMind, OPML and Markdown.

## Long description

> Othoughts turns Obsidian's Canvas into a mind-mapping tool, in the spirit of iThoughts. It works on ordinary canvas files: your cards, notes, images and links stay what they are, and the plugin adds structure on top of the arrows you draw.
>
> **Build maps from the keyboard.** Tab adds a child, Enter adds a sibling, and the new card is ready to type in. Arrow keys move along the map.
>
> **Work with whole branches.** Collapse and expand any branch from a button on the card. Drag a card and its branch moves with it; drop it next to another card to re-attach it as a child or a sibling.
>
> **Keep it tidy.** Arrange a branch or the whole map to the right, to the left, downward, upward or on both sides of the root, and choose how much each canvas rearranges itself. While you drag, arrows switch to the sides that face each other, and smooth curves replace the tight hooks between nearby cards.
>
> **Make it yours.** Seven map styles and seven backgrounds, chosen independently; a handle to scale a card and its text together; larger text for the root and the main branches.
>
> **See the whole map as an outline**, including collapsed branches, and edit it there.
>
> **Keep its history.** Snapshots save the map as it is at a given moment, by hand or automatically once a day. A timeline shows what changed between versions; snapshots open read-only and can be restored.
>
> **Take it elsewhere.** Export to FreeMind (.mm), which iThoughts, XMind, Freeplane, MindManager, MindNode and SimpleMind open, to OPML, or to a Markdown outline.
>
> No network access, no telemetry, no default hotkeys. English and Spanish interface.

## Screenshots (later)

Up to 5 desktop images of 1200 × 800 px (JPEG, PNG or WebP, 5 MB at most each). Worth having, in this order:

1. A finished map with the *Paper* or *Classic* style, both sides of the root.
2. The tools panel open (the gear at the bottom left).
3. The outline panel next to the canvas.
4. The map history panel with a few snapshots.
5. A collapsed branch with its count, next to the same branch expanded.

Use a map with neutral content (not personal notes), and take them at 100 % zoom.
